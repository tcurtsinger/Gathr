# The API: tasks, pauses, archive and the agent folder

Part of [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)'s Run, use and check it; the other parts are listed there. The API's address and sign-in are in [api.md](api.md).

- Tasks and waits ([0010](decisions/0010-coordination-as-state.md)): agents give and settle them only through Gathr's tools ([api-approvals.md](api-approvals.md)). `GET /api/chats/<id>/tasks` lists the chat's tasks (each with `given_in`, the turn that gave it) and its waits; `POST /api/tasks/<n>/cancel {"why"}` and `POST /api/tasks/<n>/restart` are the person's. Each turn in `GET /api/chats/<id>` carries its `task`, and what Gathr told an agent (a task, a wake-up, a hear-back) is a message with role `gathr`. A `wait` with `pr` and `up_to_date: true` is met once that PR has every commit its base has (GitHub's compare: `behind_by` 0), or it merges or closes (0043 C12; kind `up_to_date`). A wait that isn't met keeps what its last look saw (`seen`) and when (`looked_ts`): for CI, each check as it stands, "1 of 2 checks on #5 at 0123456 finished: 1 passed; queued: UI (macOS)"; for a PR, that it is open; for an up-to-date wait, how far behind; for a check, its exit code and what it printed (its first 300 characters, with secrets hidden). A wait of several parts gives each part's. `list_tasks` shows it to the wait's agent as "Last look: …", with the error of looks that failed. A CI check is queued, running, passed, failed, cancelled, skipped or unknown (a conclusion Gathr has no word for, never counted as passed), and the wake names the ones that failed, were cancelled or are unknown: a job GitHub cancelled isn't a failed test (G144, G181). CI has finished once every check has, whatever each came to. The service looks at open CI, PR, up-to-date and check waits, and tasks' PRs, every 2 minutes; `GATHR_WAIT_EVERY_MS` in the service's environment shortens that, in dev and tests only. `gathr diag health` lists open tasks and waits; events are `gathr.task.*` and `gathr.wait.*`. To try it without a login, hold the replay engine (`GATHR_REPLAY_HOLD`), take a token from `GATHR_REPLAY_ENV`, and call the tools as in `tests/coordination.rs`; a recording's `{"dir": "hold", "line": "take"}` row uses the hold file up, so each one you make lets one held turn on, for a round of several agents in order. `cargo test --test <name>` doesn't rebuild the replay engine: `cargo build --example replay_engine` after changing it.
- The person's tasks (F9, [0010](decisions/0010-coordination-as-state.md)): only a chat's Supervisor (or a chat's only agent) gives them, with `ask_person`; from a member it becomes a `person_request` task for the Supervisor, who files it (`for_task`), folds it into one the person has, or answers it, and passes the answer back with `finish_task`. A person task has `owner: null`, kind `person`, a `form` (`done`, `yesno`, `choice`, `choices`, `text`), `choices` (`label`, `detail`; the asker's pick first) and `files` (copies made when asked, read at `GET /api/tasks/<n>/files/<i>`, served with `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox`). `GET /api/person-tasks` (`?chat=<id>` for one chat) lists the open ones, oldest first, each with `chat_title` and `for_agent`; `POST /api/person-tasks/answer {"answers": [{"task", "pick", "yes", "done", "text"}]}` takes each answer or refuses it on its own (`{"answered", "refused": [{"task", "why", "ref"}]}`); words alone answer any form. Each Send wakes each asker once (`gathr.task.answered`), and each task it settled gets that wake-up's turn as `heard_in`, where the chat shows "You answered #<n> · <answer>" on one line, whole with the question on click, as a hand-off line shows its task's `why` (a `give_task` title is at most 80 characters); a refusal is `gathr.task.answer`.
- Pause and stop (F10, [0010](decisions/0010-coordination-as-state.md)): `GET /api/pauses` lists each pause (`scope` `app`, `chat` or `agent`, its `target` id, `since_us`, `state` `pausing` while a turn, background command or wait check in it still runs, then `paused`, or `stopped` after Stop now, and what still `running`) and each account's usage hold (`until_us`, `kind`, its `agents`). `POST /api/pauses {"scope", "target"}` pauses; `POST /api/pauses/resume` resumes, which is Restart after a stop; `POST /api/pauses/stop` is Stop now: it interrupts the scope's turns, ends their engines' background commands with Claude Code's own `stop_task`, and queues a carry-on per agent that waits for Restart. A stopped turn's task stays as it was, `working`, with the reason `Stopped by you. Carries on at Restart`, and its giver hears nothing until the carry-on ends ([0047](decisions/0047-a-report-is-a-record.md)); only if the carry-on can't be saved is the task `stopped`. A pause holds the turns Gathr queues (tasks, wake-ups, hear-backs) and its waits' looks and checks, never the person's own message; a held turn stays `queued` with `held` (`pause` or `usage`) and its `why`, and all of it outlasts a restart. `GET /api/chats/<id>` adds `run` (the chat's own pause or the app's, else `{"state": "running"}`) and, on each member, `paused` and `usage_until_us`. A `rate_limit_event` with status `rejected` holds the account until 30 s after its reset: a turn that ran no tool is sent again then, one that did ends and a carry-on waits, its task left as it was with the reason `The account is out of usage. Carries on when it is back` (`tests/frames/usage_limit.jsonl` is made by hand: no limit was hit live). When a background command ends, Claude Code starts a turn by itself: it gets its row, on the task of the turn that started the command, and a task stays `held` while one runs. When one ends while its engine runs another turn, Claude Code says so inside that turn and begins none of its own, so the task held on it settles there and then if its owner reported it done (G174), and otherwise its owner is woken on it; with the engine idle, the turn it begins settles it. Its owner may `finish_task` a held task from any turn: the report is kept, and the task is done once the hold ends, at once if nothing is left open. An engine that exits takes its commands with it: a task held on them carries on. A turn with no progress for 10 minutes (20 during a tool) is flagged once (`gathr.turn.stalled`, live `{"type": "stalled"}`), never stopped; `GATHR_STALL_AFTER_MS` shortens that, in dev and tests only. Events are `gathr.pause.*` and `gathr.usage.hold`.
- Archive and delete (F11, [0010](decisions/0010-coordination-as-state.md)): `POST /api/chats/<id>/archive` ends the chat's work in one step (its open tasks cancelled with why, waits ended, queued turns stopped, its pause gone, one `gathr.chat.archive` event), then interrupts its running turns and ends their background commands; it answers `{"archived", "ended": {"tasks", "waits", "turns"}}`. A send, a task or a restart in an archived chat is refused (409, `the chat is archived`). A turn archiving stopped stays stopped: a start under way then (its engine's version or login being read) drops it rather than leave it waiting for a sign-in, and an ended turn never goes back to `queued` or `waiting_sign_in`. `POST …/unarchive` puts it back in the list and starts nothing. `DELETE /api/chats/<id>` archives it first, closes its engines the gentle way, removes its rows, then its worktrees (their branches stay), hooks and task files: `{"deleted", "left"}`, `left` naming what couldn't be removed, with its reference. Archiving also removes the chat's worktrees in the agent folder (below), which a restored chat's next turn checks out again from their branches, but keeps a worktree with changes not committed, and a chat's folder outside a project, until the chat is deleted. Each sends `{"type": "chat", "chat", "change"}` live (`archived`, `unarchived`, `deleted`).
- Reports ([0047](decisions/0047-a-report-is-a-record.md)): a task keeps its owner's last report, and Gathr works out where the task stands from it. `finish_task {"how", "task", "note", "pr", "commit", "file", "on_task", "signed_off"}` is taken on a task in any state, from any turn of its owner's: while a turn runs on the task it applies when that turn ends (`Recorded: done. It applies when the turn running on task #<n> ends.`), otherwise at once (`Recorded: done. Task #<n> is <state>: <reason>.`). A done task answers `Already recorded`, and a cancelled one says so. With no `task`, it is the task the turn works on, or the caller's one open task in the chat; a task in another chat is refused. What is refused says what is missing: a done review with no `commit`, a review's `commit` that isn't 7 to 40 hex digits, done work with no PR, `commit`, `file` or `note`, a left with no `note`, a blocked with neither `note` nor `on_task`, a `pr` that isn't the task's own. Each task in `GET /api/chats/<id>/tasks` carries `report` (`how`, `note`, `see`: work's commit or file, `at_us`), `asked` (Gathr asked for a report and none has come) and `waiting_on` (what a held task has open). A turn that ends well never marks its task done: with nothing open and no report, Gathr queues one turn asking for it (`Your last turn on task #<n> (<what>) ended with no report.`), and after a second silent turn the task is `blocked` with `no report: two turns ended without one` and its giver hears. A task reported left with nothing open is `blocked` with `left: <note>`; its PR merging later wakes its owner instead of marking it done. A merge finishes work only if its owner reported it done; unreported work whose turn still runs is left to that turn's end. A review is done when its PR merges. A task Gathr blocks for the one it comes `after` gets that task as `blocked_on`, and goes back in line when it is restarted and finishes. `list_tasks` also lists the caller's own blocked, failed and stopped tasks. A hear-back names only the tasks its giver hasn't heard about; a task that settles as something other than what its giver last heard (blocked, then reported done) is heard once more, by itself; the same outcome again is not repeated, except after a restart, which its giver asked for. `give_task {"in_passing": true}` gives a task that isn't part of the giver's own: the giver's task isn't held on it, the hear-back on the others doesn't wait for it, and it is heard back on by itself (each task carries `in_passing`). Cancelling a member's request the person has already answered settles it as done with the answer, so what waited on it carries on. A review (`reviewed`, `outdated`, `pr_head` on its task) is out of date only when its PR's head has moved past the commit reviewed by more than clean merges of the base: Gathr's look asks git once for each pair of commit reviewed and head, by the merge tool's own rule, keeps that verdict for that pair only. When git can't say (a fetch fails, another git is at work), it keeps no verdict: the review reads out of date and the next look asks again. A review reported done is the word to land its PR ([0049](decisions/0049-gathr-lands-pull-requests.md), [api-checks.md](api-checks.md)): Gathr looks at once and the answer adds what it did or saw; `signed_off: true` goes only with a review that is done, and is kept on the task (`signed_off`). A reviewer records a done review again with `finish_task {"how": "done", "commit"}` on the same task while the PR is open; the task stays done, and if the commit isn't the PR's head as last seen the review reads out of date until the next look judges it. `list_tasks` shows `[reviewed at <commit>, out of date: #<pr> is at <head>]` and also lists the caller's own done reviews that went out of date. To try it: as in `tests/coordination.rs`, give a task, let its turn end, and read the task.
- Stale tasks (F11): a task nothing moved for the stale days gets `stale_us` (its last move) and its Supervisor is told once; after the cancel days stale it's cancelled (`stale since <date>`, restartable) unless something can still move it. `GET /api/settings` and `PATCH /api/settings {"stale_days", "cancel_days", "agent_folder"}` (Settings > General, 1 to 365 each, 3 and 7 by default). `GET /api/person-tasks` adds `stale`, the agents' stale tasks across chats (each with `chat_title`). The sweep runs with the service's looks; `GATHR_STALE_DAY_MS` shortens a day, in dev and tests only. A task whose `after` task or blocker is cancelled is cancelled at once. Events are `gathr.task.stale`, `gathr.chat.*` and `gathr.settings.change`.
- The agent folder ([0034](decisions/0034-agent-folder.md)): `GET /api/settings` answers `agent_folder`, a full path (unset, the home folder's `Gathr` for the system's own data folder, else `<data>-agents` beside the data folder, so a test folder, opened by `cargo dev` or by `gathr-app --data`, keeps its agents to itself); `PATCH /api/settings {"agent_folder"}` saves it, or refuses (400, with a reference) a path that isn't full, holds or is in the data folder, or can't be made or written. It applies to new work. Each project has `<agent folder>/<Project>/Shared` and `<Project>/<Agent>`, names made safe for both disks, unique, and kept across a rename (`folder_name`); a chat with no project uses `Chats`. An agent's worktree is `<Agent>/<chat>` (the chat id's last six characters), a plain chat's folder the same, and each engine's `TEMP`, `TMP` and `TMPDIR` is `<Agent>/temp/chat-<chat>`, fixed when it starts and cleared once it closes (never a restart to change it). Claude Code gets `--add-dir` for the agent's folder and Shared, Codex both as writable roots; one that holds the data folder or leads into it through a link isn't passed (`gathr.folder.refused`, with a reference), an agent folder that does (a link made after it was saved) fails the turn before anything is made, and the hook asks before any write that lands in the data folder, links followed, except in an engine's own folder from before the agent folder (`<data>/worktrees/<chat>/<agent>`, `<data>/chats/<chat>`) while no link in it leads elsewhere (one that does fails the turn the same way). Paths are as the disk names them (macOS's `/private/var`), so a test compares the engine's folders with `gathr::folders::real`. Every folder is recorded before it's made (`agent_folders`; the start event's `gathr.folder.*`); worktrees from before stay in `<data>/worktrees` for an agent with a session there. A sweep with the service's looks (and at once on archive and delete) removes a closed engine's temp folder, an archived chat's worktrees and a deleted chat's folders, never one a running engine or wait check uses, nor one whose chat was restored meanwhile: `gathr.cleanup.skip` (`in_use`, `changes`, `in_data`, `left`) with a reference, then `gathr.cleanup.remove` on the same trace. To try it: `cargo dev`, a project chat, a message; the start event's `cwd` is under `<data>-agents`; archive the chat and look for `gathr.cleanup.*` in `gathr diag failures` and `diag trace`.
- Finished PR cleanup (0043 C10): a task's merged or closed PR queues removal of its recorded agent
  worktrees and local branch. The PR looks and the merge tool both queue it; skipped work survives a
  restart and retries with the existing sweep. Dirty work, running engines/wait checks, newer commits,
  other branches and unrecorded worktrees stay. A fork's same-named local branch is never claimed.
  Events are `gathr.cleanup.skip` and `gathr.cleanup.remove`, with the branch and shared reference.
- What a chat waits on ([0050](decisions/0050-what-a-chat-waits-on.md)): `GET /api/chats/<id>/waiting` answers `lines`, `agents`, `in_words` (the same as text), `at_us` (the service's clock) and `from` (`service`). Each line has `kind` (`turn`, `queued`, `sign_in`, `approval`, `question`, `wait`, `command`, `pr`, `given`, `merge`, `after`, `idle`, `trouble`, `update`), `what` (the line in words), `on` (what is waited for, in a few words), `agent`, `task`, `since_us`, `moves` (`itself`, `you`, `nobody`, or `others` for a line that follows other lines), `from` (`saved`, or `service` for what only the running service knows) and, for a wait or a PR to land, `seen`, `looked_us` and `error`. They come sorted: what waits on you, then what nobody is on, then what moves by itself, the longest wait first in each. A queued turn says why it hasn't started: a pause (`you`), an update, usage, a missing engine (as saved on the turn), or what its agent's queue says: "behind this agent's turn on #3", "…in another chat", "its engine is closing", "its engine hasn't yet read a message sent into its last turn", "it compacts the conversation once this agent's background commands end"; with nothing said, "unknown". A wait is `nobody`'s once three looks in a row failed, and `you` under a pause. A task no turn is on says what it waits for (`after`, `given`, `merge`), that nothing is on it (`idle`: ready or working with no turn), or that it is blocked, failed or stopped and whether its giver was told (`trouble`). A PR to land says what Gathr's last look saw (`landings.saw`), `nobody`'s once it is left to its reviewer. Each agent in `agents` has `state` (`needs_you`, `working`, `waiting`, `free`), `words` ("Waiting for CI on #157 and Ann", "Held", "Paused", "Stopped", "Usage limit"), `task`, `since_us`, `on` (whom and what it waits for, each with `since_us`) and `until_us`. Live `{"type": "waiting", "chat"}` whenever the chat's answer may have changed (a task or wait, a turn queued, started, held or ended, a look that saw something new or began to fail, a background command, a look at a PR to land, an approval, a queue's reason): a window showing the chat asks again. The `waiting` tool gives an agent the same for its own chat, in words. `gathr diag waiting --chat <id>` asks the service, and with it stopped reads the database (`from`: `database`), where a running turn reads "was running when the service stopped". The `message` tool's answer names what holds the turn it queued: "once Gathr has updated", or "when Gathr", "the chat" or the agent "is resumed" (G163). To try it: as in `tests/waiting.rs` and `tests/messages.rs`.
